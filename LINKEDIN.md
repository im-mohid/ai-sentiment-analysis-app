# LinkedIn Demo Video Plan (45–60 seconds)

## Before recording
- Run the app once before recording so the first start is already done.
- Browser zoom 110–125%, close extra tabs, hide bookmarks bar, turn on Do Not Disturb.
- Record in 1080p. Easy tools: Xbox Game Bar (`Win + G`), OBS Studio, or Clipchamp.
- Keep the sample CSV in an easy-to-find folder.

## Recording sequence

| Time | What to show | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | App home page, sidebar visible | "Sentiment Analysis App · Python · Streamlit · VADER NLP" |
| 5–12 s | Click the Positive example, press **Analyze Sentiment** | "Real-time sentiment prediction" |
| 12–22 s | Do the Negative and Neutral examples quickly | "Positive · Negative · Neutral + sentiment score" |
| 22–30 s | Go to Batch Analysis, upload the sample CSV, pick the column | "Batch analysis from CSV" |
| 30–40 s | Click Analyze Reviews, show the progress bar, then the KPI cards | "Analyze many reviews at once" |
| 40–50 s | Scroll through the donut chart, bar chart, use the sentiment filter | "Interactive dashboard" |
| 50–60 s | Click Download, then show the CSV in Excel | "Export results to CSV" |
| End card | Project title + GitHub link | "Built with Python · VADER · Pandas · Plotly" |

## Editing tips
- Cut waiting time (long progress bars) or speed those parts to 2x.
- Keep captions short (max 6–8 words) and large; many people watch muted.
- Add subtle background music at low volume, or none.
- Zoom in on the result card and charts with a gentle crop instead of fancy transitions.
- Export as MP4 (1080p). A square or 4:5 crop often looks better on mobile feeds.
- Put the GitHub link in the first comment or the post text.

## LinkedIn caption

I built a Sentiment Analysis web app that reads customer reviews and classifies them as Positive, Negative or Neutral. 💬

What it does:
✅ Analyzes a single review and shows its sentiment score
✅ Processes whole CSV files of reviews in batches
✅ Shows an interactive dashboard with sentiment distribution and filters
✅ Exports the results as a CSV file

Tech stack: Python, Streamlit, VADER (a lexicon- and rule-based NLP sentiment tool), Pandas and Plotly.

What I learned:
🔹 How a lexicon-based sentiment analyzer works and how to turn its scores into labels
🔹 Why a sentiment score is not the same as accuracy
🔹 How to structure a small data project and build a dashboard in Streamlit
🔹 The limits of rule-based tools: English only, and they can struggle with sarcasm and mixed opinions

Sentiment analysis helps businesses quickly understand large volumes of customer feedback and spot problems or happy customers faster.

This is a learning project, and I'd love your feedback and suggestions for improvement! What feature would you add next?
